The Big Question

So, Is South Korea Safe?

South Korea consistently ranks among the safest countries on earth, and it holds up in practice, not just in the statistics.

Crime rates are exceptionally low, public transport is spotless and runs late, and it is entirely normal to see someone leave a laptop on a café table while they order.

Getting Around

The Subway Does Everything

Seoul’s metro is signed in English throughout, runs until well past midnight and costs about a pound a journey. You will not need a taxi.

Pick up a T-money card at any convenience store on day one — it works on the subway, the buses and, usefully, in the convenience stores themselves.

Solo Travel

Going Alone Is Normal Here

Solo dining is completely unremarkable — many restaurants have single-seat counters built for it, and there is no awkwardness about eating on your own.

Walking home late is ordinary too. Streets stay busy and well lit long after midnight, which is not something you can say of most capital cities.
