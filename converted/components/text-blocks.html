Text blocks — micro-layouts for page content

Every text box used to build a page body, in the order you’d reach for them: the column that holds the copy, the section head that opens a block, and the body blocks that go inside. Each specimen is copy-in as shown — the pink class name is the only class you need.

For a row of text beside a picture — image left, text right and back again — see media-text-rows; that is a layout rather than a text block, so it has its own file.

Sizes are written as base → sm, meaning the mobile size and the size from 640px up. They mirror the prototype’s Tailwind steps exactly, so the two builds agree at every width — don’t re-set font-size on these by hand.

1 · Columns — what holds the text

.ess-colmax-width 48rem, centred · padding 1rem → 1.5rem → 2rem. The standard reading column — use it for any run of prose.

This is the standard page text column. It stops at 48rem so a line never runs past a comfortable reading measure, and it sits in the middle of the band whatever the screen width. The dashed rule is the section edge, drawn here only to show the column sitting inside it.

.ess-col.ab-prosethe same column, with body-copy styling applied to every direct <p> — 16 → 18px, line-height 1.625, 1.25rem apart. Long-form pages use this.

Inside .ab-prose you write plain paragraphs — no class per line. The column styles them.

Every paragraph gets the same size, leading and spacing, so a story page is just a stack of <p> tags with the occasional block from section 3 dropped in.

.ab-prose--basemodifier — holds body copy at 16px with no step up at 640px. For denser index / hub columns.

Same column, one size throughout. Use it where the page is a list of links or summaries rather than something you settle in and read.

.containerfull page width. Use instead of .ess-col when the section holds a grid, cards or a carousel.

Wider than the reading column — text set this wide is hard to read, so keep .container for section heads and anything laid out in columns.

2 · Heads — what opens a section

.ess-eyebrow + .ess-h2 + .ess-p.ab-ledethe standard section head. Eyebrow 12px/.2em pink · title 30 → 36px Montserrat 900 · lede 16 → 18px capped at 42rem. Wrap words in <span> to turn them pink.

Before You Go

Travel Insurance

The lede sits under the title and sets up the section in a sentence or two. It stops at 42rem so it stays narrower than the heading above it.

.ab … .ess-h2the same markup inside <main class="ab"> — title steps up to 36 → 48px, eyebrow tightens to 11px/.3em. This is the About-page head; nothing extra to write.

What We Live By

Our Core Values

Our values are what guide us in our decision-making and how we show up — for each other and for the communities we visit.

.ess-h3sub-head, one step down — 18 → 20px Montserrat 900 uppercase. For headings inside a section.

What Good Cover Looks Like

Follow it with body copy as normal.

.ess-secspacer — 4rem below. Wrap each head-plus-body group in it to space a stack of sections evenly.

No visible styling of its own; it only sets the rhythm between blocks.

3 · Body blocks — what goes in the column

<p>standard page text — 16 → 18px, line-height 1.625, #d1d5db. Unclassed inside .ab-prose; use .ess-p anywhere else.

The default. Most of a page is this — no class needed inside the prose column.

.ess-p--sm14 → 16px. Supporting copy under a card or an aside.

A smaller size for secondary copy that shouldn’t compete with the main text.

.ab-lead24 → 30px Montserrat 900 uppercase white, line-height 1.25. The opening statement of a page. <em> inside turns pink.

A bunch of fun loving legends with a passion for travel.

.ab-strongbody size, white and bold. Lifts one paragraph out of a run without changing the rhythm.

Ordinary copy running along at the usual weight and colour.

And the line you actually want them to take away.

.ab-beat20 → 24px Montserrat 700 white, tight leading (1.25). A short beat between passages — one or two lines, not a paragraph.

Travel has the power to change lives.

.ab-beat--loosethe same size on the normal line box (28 → 32px leading). Use when the beat runs to several lines.

The same weight and size, but with room to breathe when the thought needs more than a single line to land.

.ab-closer20 → 24px Montserrat 900 uppercase pink. Closes a section — one per block at most.

And that is still why we do it.

.ab-pull36 → 48px Montserrat 900 uppercase, 2px pink rule on the left. Each <span> is one white line; <em> turns a line pink.

To make apositive difference.

.ab-handCaveat, pink, 30 → 36px. The handwritten aside — use it sparingly, once a page.

Still the best decision I ever made.

4 · Dense flow — terms, conditions, policy

.ess-terms__h + .ess-terms__pheading 18 → 20px with 2.5rem above (none on the first), body 14 → 15px. A long numbered document, not a story — tighter than the blocks above.

Our Story

How It Started

Back in 2006, I was saving for a house in London.

That was the plan. Work hard. Save money. Follow the path. And it was all coming together — I’d found a house, had the deposit ready, and was days away from signing.

Then, at the eleventh hour, it fell through.

So instead of finding another house, I booked a one-way ticket.

Travel isn’t really about the places you visit.

It’s about who you become along the way, and the people you meet while you’re busy becoming them.

To make apositive difference.

And that is still why we do it.
